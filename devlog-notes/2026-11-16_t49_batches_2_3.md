# 2026-11-16: the T49 batches 2, 3 and 4 in the game, and the clip pace

Bruno passed T49 batch 2 (round 179: Lucy guard, the lodge staff, the toll guard) and batch 3 (round 181: the lab
worker, the soldier, the fireman), then batch 4 (round 183: the power armour). Each is a passed game GLB: the T26 full mesh, the T27 bake, 2048 maps inside, 23
mixamo bones. His answer on the foot slide (round 179): "Speed up the clips". All eleven classes are T49 models now.

## What changed

- `tools/characters.sh`: six more classes in `ONE_FILE` (`lucy_guard`, `lodge_staff`, `toll_guard`, `labworker`,
  `soldier`, `fireman`). The default list has eleven classes.
- `tools/characters_clips.py`: a new field `ground` for each clip: the speed of the planted toe against the hips (source
  units a second). The figure walks without a foot slide when its root moves at `ground` x its height.
- `CreateCharacterAssets`: two new classes, `lodge_staff` (NIGHT MANAGER, 1.75 m, a civilian) and `toll_guard`
  (TOLL GUARD, 1.82 m, an enemy). `ApplyPace`: in the controller of every T49 class (a class with the map set), the walk
  and the run play at the game speed / (`ground` x the height). Lucy: the TankController speeds (walk 1.6, run 3.6,
  back 1.0 m/s). The figures: the Invector AI speeds (`Characters/ClassPace.cs`: walk 2.0, run 3.0 m/s, the vendor
  defaults that `InvectorEnemyFactory` keeps). The speeds do not change. A T19 class plays at 1.
- `InvectorEnemyFactory`: the kinds `toll_guard_pistol` (60 HP, the guard's rank) and `lodge_staff` (an unarmed
  civilian, like the nurse). `BuildT49EnemyPrefabs` builds again only the enemy prefabs of the T49 classes (15 kinds).
  An enemy prefab keeps the mesh of the model it was built from, so after a model swap the old prefab had no body.
  `Resources/Enemies/` is not in git: run the build on each machine after a model swap.
- The demo (`Demo.unity`, through `DemoWorld.ApplyRound179Kinds`; the room builders set the same kinds): R3's
  `Guard_Booth` and its spare are `toll_guard_pistol`, G19's `Night_Manager` is `lodge_staff`.
- `DummyShooter.KillerIdFor`: the toll guard uses the guard's death screen row until the writer gives it its own.
- The lab worker's suit keeps its baked gloss (round 181: smoothness median 0.77, metal 0). The material uses the map as
  baked, so no code change. The import test checks that the gloss stays.

## The gas soldier's left hand (Bruno: "go ahead with the fix")

The bot `holds` in AI TEST: `Gasser_D`'s left wrist stopped 7.2 cm from its target on the gas launcher (limit 5 cm; the T19
soldier 3.4 cm). There is no shield on him. The measure (new numbers in the bot's log):

| Figure | Left arm (shoulder to wrist) | Shoulder to the wrist's target | Gap before | Left shoulder forward | Gap after |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gasser_D (launcher) | 0.498 m | 0.569 m | 7.2 cm | 20 degrees | 0.8 to 0.9 cm |
| Soldier_A (rifle) | 0.498 m | 0.523 m | 2.6 cm | 9 degrees | 0 |
| Armour_C (launcher) | 1.059 m | 0.807 m | 0 | 0 | 0 |

The cause is the arm length against the grip point. The left arm, fully straight, was short: the IK target, the hand frame
and the grip point were not the cause (the right hand and the armour's left hand sit at 0, and the grip point sits on the
pump: the pump runs from 9.8 cm behind it to 5.4 cm ahead). The T49 soldier's arm is 0.498 m from the shoulder joint to
the wrist, the T19 soldier's 0.527 m (the T49 hand joint sits nearer the fingers), and his shoulder joint sits 1 cm farther
out. The launcher's grip is 0.545 m ahead of the butt, the rifle's 0.526 m, and lower. The reach used only the upper arm
and the forearm.

The fix (`EnemyGun.Protract`): on a long gun, when the wrist's target is past the straight arm, the left clavicle turns
toward it, only as far as needed and at most 20 degrees (about 6 cm of reach on the 0.18 m clavicle), as a shooter's
support shoulder comes forward. An arm that reaches does not change (the armour: 0 degrees). PROPOSED (the 20 degrees).
The left wrist is now on the pump (0.2 cm ahead of the grip point; before, about 7 cm behind it, under the receiver).

## Open points

1. The AI body plays the Invector animator (`Invector@ShooterMelee.controller`), not the class controller. So in the
   game the walking enemies play the vendor clips, and the pace above shows only where the class controller plays (the
   figures that stand, the death screen, the bot). For the T49 clips in the AI walk, the vendor animator must take them:
   that is a separate job.
2. A walk at x1.7 to x2.0 can look fast. The other choice is a lower AI walk speed (Bruno's call).
3. The passed Lucy guard GLB has six clips: `idle`, `walk`, `run` in place and a second set (`*.001`) that travels.
   The game uses the set in place, as for Lucy bare.
4. THE POWER ARMOUR RIG IS TURNED (round 183 file): see above. In the game the body faces the root (`TurnedRigs`); the
   walk still steps about 12 degrees off the body. A new rig upstream removes it (RFab rig, the production chat).
5. The same rig binds the armour's knees bent (15 and 21 degrees). `RagdollTests.TheKnees` took the bind pose as the
   straight leg and failed (the left knee "15.7 past straight", but 4.9 degrees bent, the same as the straight leg of its
   own clips). The test now takes 0 as the straight leg when the bind pose is bent.
6. The armour's lowest vertex is 3.2 cm under the floor in its idle. The bot's limit is 3 cm, or 1.2 % of a taller class
   (3.5 cm at 2.90 m).
7. The armour at 2.90 m does not fit the room's play camera in the `lucy` bot (the head is out of the frame), and the
   `holds` bot's body shots of `Armour_C` aim at 1.3 m (its legs). The close shots show it.
